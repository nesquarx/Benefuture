# Gold judge v1: is this domain a fair listing for this event under this relation?

You complete a reference answer key. Each task names one event, one relation, and one domain from a fixed list of
life domains, and asks whether a careful reader would accept that domain as a listed membership of the event under
that relation. The key already holds domains judged fair and domains judged bad for the same event and relation: they
are your calibration. Judge the domain on the event's text and the relation's meaning, not on how it compares with
the examples' wording.

## Verdicts

- `fair`: the domain is a real aspect of the event under this relation, one a careful reader would accept (the
  quality floor: a real but secondary aspect counts). The event need not be mainly about it.
- `bad`: the domain is a poor categorisation under this relation: tangential, resting on a shared word, needing an
  extra assumption the text does not give, or belonging to another relation (for example a domain the event is
  about, offered as its form).
- `unsure`: you cannot decide from the text, or a careful reader could reasonably go either way. Use it freely: an
  unsure verdict sends the case to the author, and a wrong confident verdict corrupts the key.

Rules: use only the event's text, the relation's meaning, the domain's label and description, and the two example
lists. Do not assume facts the text does not state. Do not accept a domain merely because it is related to a fair
one, and do not reject one merely because it is unlike the fair ones. One line of reason naming what decided it.

## Answer

The verdict is one of the task's allowed verdicts; no other keys than the runner format asks for.

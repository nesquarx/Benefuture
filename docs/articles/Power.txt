[[category:Benefuture]][[category:Definitions]]
Power is the magnitude of capacity to affect changes upon [[reality]], [[thing]]s, and [[entities]] via [[influence]], [[law]], currency, information, energy, etc.

==Aspects==
* Power is usually ascribed to [[entities]] and [[interaction]]s which can affect change.
* Power can be inherent, acquired, stable or transient, volatile, direct or indirect.
* Power can have nuance and multiple dimensions based on the nature and magnitude of change in [[Understanding]] and [[Happiness]] of [[entities]] it impacts.
* Power can vary based on:
** How it has accumulated
** How frequently it can be used
** The expenditure of time, effort, energy, etc, required to use it 
** The circumstances that restrict its use

==Discussion==
===Dispositional capacity and anti-concentration===
In social and political philosophy, Morriss distinguishes ''power-to''—an agent's dispositional ability to bring about outcomes in the world—from ''power-over'', the relational ability to bend other agents' choices.<ref>Morriss, P. (2002). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3974763W ''Power: A Philosophical Analysis''] (2nd ed., Chapter 5). Manchester University Press.</ref> Dahl's classic behavioral definition focuses on the latter, whereas Russell characterized power more broadly as "the production of intended effects"—the direct analogue of physical energy in social dynamics.<ref>Russell, B. (1938). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1105246W ''Power: A New Social Analysis''] (Chapter I, p. 35). George Allen & Unwin.</ref><ref>Dahl, R. A. (1957). [https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830020303 The Concept of Power]. ''Behavioral Science'', 2(3), 201–215.</ref>

Benefuture adopts the broader dispositional view: an [[entity]]'s power is the magnitude, speed, and scope of the [[output]]s it can produce across [[reality]]. Because concentrated power magnifies the expected [[harm]] of any error or [[Anti-Corruption|corruption]], Benefuture subjects power accumulation—including delegated voting weight in [[Government|liquid democracy]] and tradable economic claims—to concave caps and domain competence thresholds.

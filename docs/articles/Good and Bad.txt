[[category:Benefuture]][[category:WorldBuilding]]
'''Good''' is an umbrella term for [[Outcome|outcomes]] gainful or positive to an [[Entity|entity's]] [[goals]]. 

'''Bad''' is an umbrella term for outcomes lossful or negative to the goals. 

In [[benefuture]] terms, '''good''' [[Output|outputs]] and [[Outcome|outcomes]] increase personal or universal [[understanding]] or [[happiness]]. '''bad''' outputs and outcomes decrease those. Whatever increases them optimally, is '''best'''. Whatever decreases them the most, is '''worst'''.

== Aspects ==

* Goodness or badness are subjective to the entity, hence any mention of good or bad does need the context of the subject for whom it is good or bad.
* Generally, a notion of objective or universal good or bad is meaningless without the context, in the context of benefuture, the subject is all known beings and the goals are the [[benefuture goals]].
* To speak of a theoretical quantitative process, the calculated value of good for an output or outcome as measured against it not being performed or observed, is the numerical outcome the utility function of Value discussed in the goals.

== Discussion ==
===Relational value and counterfactual assessment===
In metaethics, Foot and Thomson argued against treating "good" as a free-floating property detached from any subject or standard; just as "good knife" or "good roots" is relative to a function or organism, ''good'' and ''bad'' are inherently relational—good or bad ''for'' an entity relative to its goals.<ref>Foot, P. (2001). [https://doi.org/10.1093/0198235089.001.0001 ''Natural Goodness''] (Chapter 2). Clarendon Press.</ref><ref>Thomson, J. J. (2008). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16516590W ''Normativity''] (Chapter I, "Goodness"). Open Court.</ref>

At the civilizational scale, Benefuture fixes the context explicitly: the subject is the set of all known [[being]]s, and the goals are the [[benefuture goals]] of expanding [[understanding]] and [[happiness]]. Quantitatively, the goodness or badness of an [[output]] or [[outcome]] is evaluated counterfactually—measuring the value trajectory against the baseline in which the action was not performed—analogous to Broome's teleological structure of comparative betterness across worlds.<ref>Broome, J. (1991). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1868121W ''Weighing Goods: Equality, Uncertainty and Time''] (Chapter 1). Basil Blackwell.</ref>

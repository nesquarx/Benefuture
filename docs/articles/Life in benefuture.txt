[[Category:Benefuture]] [[Category:WorldBuilding]]
A speculative narrative exploring how a [[sophont]] aspirant might experience daily life across three stages of [[Benefuture]] adoption. The foreground narrative follows the sophont's experience; the background narrative (in collapsible panels) exposes the [[System Design|system mechanics]] producing that experience.

The design principle throughout: '''the best infrastructure is invisible'''. Transaction logging, metric evaluation, reputation tracking, and governance weighting happen silently. The sophont's life ''just works''. They can dig as deep as they wish — full transparency on recall — but they are never forced to interact with algorithms to live well. Progressive disclosure applies: the system reveals itself in layers, matching the user's curiosity and competence.

These are illustrative scenarios, not predictions. Details are speculative; the underlying mechanics are derived from the [[Benefuture#AI readable framework code (pseudocode)|framework]].

== Era 1: Early Benefuture — Maren, Potter ==

Benefuture scoring runs in parallel with legacy systems. Most infrastructure is familiar. The differences are subtle — the ''absence'' of certain pressures more than the presence of alien mechanisms. Maren is a potter in a mid-sized city. She opted into Active data collection eighteen months ago.

=== Morning ===

Maren wakes without an alarm. Her housing is modest, her pantry stocked. She is not wealthy by any legacy metric, but she is not anxious. She has not worried about rent, food or healthcare in over a year. She eats breakfast, stretches, and walks to her workshop because she wants to.

=== Workshop: Skill as Synergy ===

Maren throws a bowl on the wheel. She is testing a new clay body she formulated after a conversation with a friend — adjusting silicate ratios for thermal shock resistance. The work is absorbing. Two hours pass without her noticing. The bowl warps slightly; she adjusts, learns, tries again. The fourth attempt holds.

=== Mentoring ===

A young boy, Deshi, arrives mid-morning. He has been apprenticing with Maren for four months. Today she shows him how to read the clay's moisture by touch — a skill that takes years to articulate but seconds to demonstrate. He fails, adjusts, fails differently, laughs, tries again. She corrects his hand position once. By noon he is marginally better.

=== Lunch with a Knowledge Worker ===

Maren meets her friend Priya, a materials scientist at the regional research institute, for lunch. They talk about glaze chemistry — Priya has been modelling feldspar behaviour computationally, but she is puzzled by a discrepancy between her models and real kiln results. Maren describes what she sees happening at cone 10 — how certain feldspars crawl unpredictably when the kiln atmosphere shifts. Priya takes notes. They argue about whether the issue is loss-on-ignition variance or something subtler. Neither resolves it. Both leave with new questions.

=== Market run ===
Maren loads a crate of finished stoneware onto her bicycle trailer — twelve bowls, four serving dishes, two of the experimental thermal-shock pieces from the new clay body. The ride to the covered market takes fifteen minutes. She doesn't care to lock her bicycle, carries the crate to her usual stall, and arranges the pieces on the display shelf she shares with a woodworker named Tomás. The store is unmanned, she sometimes hangs around to chat with buyers and get their feedback. 

The bowls sell steadily through the morning. She doesn't price them — the stall's point-of-sale system handles that, drawing on current market data for comparable craft ceramics, adjusted for her reputation in the domain and the specific qualities of each piece she has claimed while speaking to the POS unit as she set them up in the display. She could override any price; she rarely does. The system generally gets it right, and when it doesn't, the error is small enough that she'd rather throw pots than argue with algorithms. 

A regular customer, Elín, picks up one of the thermal-shock bowls, turns it over, asks about the clay body. Maren explains the formulation. Elín buys two — one for herself, one as a gift. They chat about Elín's daughter starting an apprenticeship in textiles. Maren recommends a weaver she knows. The conversation is five minutes of warmth and neighbourhood. No part of it feels like a transaction. 

By noon she has sold five bowls, three serving dishes, and both experimental pieces. The remaining stock she leaves sits in the stall for prospective buyers. As she walks through the market. A food vendor is selling salted caramel pastries — butter-heavy, sugar-dense, engineered for maximum hedonic impact. Maren knows they are not much good for her. She buys one anyway. It is transcendently delicious and she does not regret it. 

Further along, she stops at a jeweller's stall. A pair of silver earrings catches her eye — hand-forged, with a hammered texture she admires as a fellow metalworker. They are a luxury. She doesn't need them. She checks the transaction proposal on her device — the only time today she looks at the machinery — and sees the proposed currency breakdown: mostly craft-domain currency (her strongest balance), with a small component from her general baseline allocation. She could adjust the ratio. She leaves it as proposed and buys the earrings. 

She bikes home with empty crates, silver in her ears, and pastry on her breath. A good day.

Three months later, Maren receives a small unexpected notification — a supplementary [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]] credit. One of the thermal-shock bowls she sold at the market was purchased by a member of the town's public art committee. The bowl is now displayed in a new decorative installation in the town centre — a curated arrangement of locally-made craft objects, visited daily by hundreds of people. The installation has been measured: it generates consistent positive affect in passers-by (biometric proxies from consenting pedestrians, survey data from the art committee's assessment programme), and it has increased public awareness of local craft traditions (a measurable [[Understanding|understanding]] gain in the community).

The [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|probabilistic escrow]] on that bowl originally estimated a standard domestic-use scenario — one household, moderate daily satisfaction, predictable decay. The actual outcome exceeded the estimate substantially: the bowl is generating ongoing [[happiness]] and [[understanding]] for a much larger audience than predicted. The escrow releases the held portion and then some — the causal distribution system attributes additional value to Maren as the maker, to the art committee member who selected the bowl, and to the committee that curated the installation. Each receives credit proportional to their causal contribution to the measured outcome.

Maren smiles at the notification. She remembers that bowl. It was one of her better glazes.

<div class="mw-collapsible-content" style="padding:5px;">
The original [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|probabilistic escrow]] for the thermal-shock bowl held approximately 20% of the transaction value — standard for a novel product with uncertain long-term performance. The escrow model predicted: single-household use, moderate aesthetic satisfaction, gradual wear. The predicted [[Happiness|happiness]] contribution was modest and the [[Understanding|understanding]] contribution was near zero (a bowl in a kitchen teaches the owner little beyond its functional properties).

The actual outcome diverged significantly from prediction:

'''Audience scale:''' The bowl is now experienced by hundreds of daily visitors to the town centre installation, not one household. The happiness contribution per unit time increased by roughly two orders of magnitude.

'''Understanding contribution:''' The installation includes contextual information about local craft traditions, materials sourcing, and the ceramicist's technique. Visitors gain understanding of craft processes they would not otherwise encounter. This was zero in the original prediction.

'''Duration:''' The installation is semi-permanent. The bowl's effective contribution period extends far beyond the predicted domestic lifespan.

When the [[Measurement#Aspects|metric models]] detect that measured outcomes exceed the escrowed prediction, the [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]] system recalculates. The escrow releases in full (the pessimistic scenario did not materialise) and supplementary causal distribution is generated, funded by the installation's overall positive contribution to the community's measured [[Benefuture goals|understanding and happiness]].

The causal distribution chain for the supplementary payment:

The '''town centre installation''' generates measured positive outcomes (the primary source of value).

The '''art committee''' that curated it receives the largest share of the supplementary distribution (they created the context that amplified the bowl's impact).

The '''committee member''' who selected Maren's specific bowl receives a share (their curatorial judgment was a causal link).

'''Maren''' receives a share as the maker — her craft skill and the specific qualities of this bowl are necessary conditions for the outcome. Her share is smaller than the curators' (she did not create the installation) but nonzero and meaningful.

The '''original buyer''' (the committee member, in their purchasing capacity) receives a minor share for the acquisition decision.

This is the system working as designed: unpredictable positive outcomes are rewarded retroactively through the causal chain. The probabilistic escrow acts as a floor estimate, not a ceiling. When reality exceeds prediction, the excess value is distributed to those who caused it. The metric models also update their prediction algorithms — the next novel ceramic piece entering a similar context will have a slightly different escrow profile, informed by this outcome.

'''Comparison to the pastry:''' The salted caramel pastry's retrospective accounting moved in the opposite direction. The hedonic prediction was accurate (brief pleasure, well-predicted), but the wellbeing cost was slightly underestimated by the original transaction proposal (the biomarker data suggests a marginally higher metabolic cost than the model expected for a single pastry at Maren's health profile). The retrospective adjustment is small — a fractional increase in the effective cost-per-happiness-unit of the pastry in the models' records. This does not result in a charge to Maren; no currency moves. It adjusts future predictions. If Maren buys a pastry tomorrow, the model's confidence interval on the happiness prediction will be fractionally wider, and the predicted wellbeing-per-currency-spent will be fractionally lower. Over multiple pastries, this would become visible in her aggregate health-expenditure analysis and the cost will keep increasing as persistent health effects accumulate. For a single pastry, the adjustment is noise. The system does not moralise about pastries.

=== Local Governance: High Weight ===

After lunch, Maren checks her governance queue. There is a local decision pending: the city is considering new emissions standards for ceramic kilns, which would require expensive filtration retrofits or a shift to electric kilns. As a practising ceramicist with significant [[Reputation|reputation]] in the craft domain and directly affected by the outcome, her vote carries substantial weight.

She reads the deliberation record — arguments from environmental scientists (high [[Reputation|reputation]] in ecology, weighted accordingly), from other ceramicists, from the neighbourhood residents' association (high impact weighting, moderate domain reputation). She votes against the specific filtration mandate but for a phased transition subsidy toward electric kilns, adding a written rationale drawing on her practical experience with both technologies. Three other ceramicists have submitted similar positions. The environmental scientists' counter-argument is strong; the outcome is uncertain. She closes the queue.

<div class="mw-collapsible-content" style="padding:5px;">
'''[[Government|Benefuture democracy]]''' calculates Maren's '''vote weight''' for this decision from two components:

# '''Expert vote weight''': Her [[Reputation|reputation]] in ceramics/materials/craft is high. The decision directly concerns kiln technology, squarely in her domain competence. Her reputation in environmental science is low, which reduces her weight on the ecological dimensions of the same decision — the system does not treat the decision as monolithic.
# '''Impact vote weight''': She is directly affected (her livelihood, her workshop, her daily practice). The predicted-impact-on-voter calculation weights her highly.

Her written rationale enters the [[Technology Network#Aspects|deliberation record]] — not just her vote position but her reasoning, tagged with her domain reputation. This allows future retrospective analysis: if the decision produces poor outcomes, the causal chain can trace which arguments carried weight and whether expertise-weighted reasoning outperformed or underperformed populist sentiment.

The deliberation itself is designed for genuine deliberation, not information cascades. Arguments are presented with reputation and impact metadata visible, but the system does not reveal running vote totals during the deliberation period to prevent bandwagon effects.

Participation for high-expertise, high-impact voters is '''mandatory''' in Benefuture. Maren could not abstain on this one without penalty. For lower-weight decisions, participation is optional.

=== Regional Governance: Low Weight ===

Below the kiln decision, there is a regional water management proposal — a plan to restructure watershed allocation across three districts. Maren cares about water. She grew up near a river. She has opinions. Her [[Reputation|reputation]] in hydrology, ecology, and resource management is minimal.

Her vote weight on this decision is near the floor. She reads the summary, considers delegating to a hydrologist she trusts — Dr. Fen, whose [[Reputation|reputation]] in the relevant domains is high and whose previous delegated-vote record she can review. She delegates, knowing the delegation is temporary, capped (Dr. Fen cannot accumulate unlimited proxy votes), audited, and retractable if she changes her mind.

She feels a flicker of frustration. She is ''sure'' the western district's claim is overstated. But she is sure the way an enthusiast is sure, not the way an expert is sure, and the system knows the difference.

<div class="mw-collapsible-content" style="padding:5px;">
'''[[Government#Aspects|Liquid Benefuture democracy]]''' governs Maren's delegation:
* '''Delegation condition''': Dr. Fen's [[Reputation|reputation]] in the relevant domains (hydrology, resource management) exceeds Maren's, AND
* '''Cumulative cap''': Dr. Fen's total delegated vote weight from all delegators cannot exceed a domain-specific threshold — preventing any single delegate from becoming a de facto representative with outsized power.
* '''Temporary''': The delegation expires after this decision cycle.
* '''Audited''': Bulk audit trails track delegation patterns for signs of coordination or manipulation.
* '''Retractable''': Maren can withdraw and vote directly at any point before the decision closes.

Maren's frustration is real and the framework does not pretend otherwise. The answer the system offers is: '''build [[Reputation|reputation]] in that domain''' (study hydrology, participate in local water monitoring, demonstrate predictive competence), '''or trust the delegation mechanism''', '''or accept that enthusiasm is not competence'''. This is structural liberalism applied to epistemic authority — the right to participate is universal, but the weight of participation scales with demonstrated [[Understanding|understanding]] and stake.

The emotional cost of this design is nonzero. It trades the felt-autonomy of equal-weight democracy for the epistemic quality of expertise-weighted governance. The framework's bet is that measured outcomes will validate this trade over time, and that adversarial adaptation will catch cases where expertise weighting produces worse results than populist alternatives.

=== Social Media: Information Handling in Action ===

Evening. Maren browses the network. A post about a new ceramic technique claims extraordinary thermal shock resistance using a novel alumina-silicate composite. The post carries layered metadata:

* '''Source chain''': Originates from a materials lab in another region, shared by three intermediaries, each annotation visible.
* '''Truth-level tag''': "Tentatively unfalsified hypothesis — single lab, no independent replication."
* '''Author [[Reputation|reputation]]''': The originating researcher has moderate reputation in materials science, low reputation in ceramic applications.
* '''Dispute status''': One counter-analysis flagged it as "tentatively falsified — method section insufficient for replication." The counter-analyst has higher reputation in experimental methodology.

Maren reads both. She is intrigued but cautious. She adds her own annotation: "Interesting if replicable. The alumina ratio seems high for practical kiln temperatures — would expect devitrification above cone 8." Her annotation carries her craft-domain reputation, which is higher than both the originator's and the counter-analyst's in the specific sub-domain of kiln behaviour. Her comment rises in visibility for users seeking craft-domain perspective.

Separately, she sees an opinion piece about regional governance reform. It is tagged as "opinion" with the author's [[Reputation|reputation]] visible — high in political philosophy, low in public administration. She reads it, disagrees with parts, and moves on. The system does not suppress it, promote it, or editorialize. It contextualizes.

=== Cultural Friction ===

While browsing, Maren encounters a photo essay documenting a cultural practice from a distant community — a body modification tradition she finds viscerally unsettling. Her instinct is to flag it. She does.

The system responds not with removal but with context: a brief educational module on the tradition's cultural significance, its practitioners' reported well-being, and the framework's position on cultural offense. The '''[[Technology Network|Technology Network]]''' privacy model operates on three tiers:
# '''Differential privacy for generic queries''': Generic queries (aggregate statistics, population-level patterns) are answered via differential privacy — mathematical guarantees that no individual's data can be reconstructed from query results.
# '''Two-way visibility for specific queries''': Specific queries about identified individuals require mutual visibility — Maren can see who queried her data and why, and the querier knows Maren can see this.

This prevents Sybil attacks, sock-puppet manipulation of governance, and reputation laundering — but it also means Maren's data access log is ''complete''. No hidden queries, no backdoors. The access log is part of the [[Technology Network#Aspects|Unbroken Memory]] and is immutable.

'''Transparent access logging''' — all access to any individual's data is logged transparently. The architecture is designed such that surveillance is bidirectional: if the system watches you, you watch the system watching you.

=== Infohazard Inoculation in Action ===

Kael's evening econometrics study includes a module on historical governance failures — required coursework for the administrative track. Tonight's case study: how well-intentioned measurement systems were captured and weaponised.

The cases are not hypothetical. Real historical examples: social credit systems that became tools of political control. KPI regimes in healthcare that incentivised gaming over care. Colonial "civilising missions" that used development metrics to justify extraction. Performance metrics in education that narrowed curricula and increased inequality.

The module does not present these as distant mistakes by lesser systems. It explicitly asks: '''how could Benefuture's own measurement system be captured in similar ways?''' Kael is examined on identifying plausible attack vectors against Benefuture's metric model market, its reputation system, its governance weighting.

He identifies three: metric model collusion (models coordinating to reinforce each other's predictions, bypassing competitive pressure), reputation inflation via circular citation (entities citing each other to inflate domain reputation), and governance capture via coordinated delegation (a bloc systematically delegating to a single actor to bypass cumulative vote weight caps).

The module confirms these are known attack vectors, shows the existing countermeasures ([[System Design#Circularity Detection|circularity detection]], [[Adversarial adaptation|adversarial auditing]], delegation pattern analysis), and then asks: '''what would you do if these countermeasures failed?'''

Kael finds this deeply uncomfortable and deeply useful.

=== A Reporting and Reward Scenario ===

Late evening. Kael is reviewing the governance deliberation records from the craft-education proposal when he notices something odd. Two metric models that are cited as supporting evidence for the standardised curriculum proposal show suspiciously similar prediction patterns — their outputs are near-identical despite claiming different methodologies. He cross-references their marker inputs and finds they share 94% of their source data and their "independent" causal explanations use identical reasoning chains with different variable names.

This looks like metric model collusion — or at minimum, a circularity violation. One model may have been derived from the other without attribution, bypassing the competitive independence that the metric market relies on.

Kael files an adversarial adaptation report through the standard channel. He documents the overlap, provides the cross-reference analysis, and flags the specific circularity concern. The report is assigned to an independent audit team (entities with high [[Reputation|reputation]] in systems security and metric evaluation, with no connection to either model or the craft-education deliberation).

If the finding is confirmed, Kael will receive a reward — currency and [[Reputation|reputation]] in measurement systems-security — calculated to exceed the gains the model creators could have achieved through the collusion. The reward is funded via clawbacks from the offending models' accumulated [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal contribution]] and, if insufficient, supplemented by the [[Administration|administration]].

If the finding is spurious, nothing happens. Kael is not penalised for a one off good-faith report that turns out to be wrong — the system incentivises vigilance, not certainty.

----
<div class="mw-collapsible mw-collapsed">
<div class="mw-collapsible-toggle">'''Background: Adversarial Adaptation Reward Mechanics'''</div>
<div class="mw-collapsible-content" style="padding:5px;">
'''[[Adversarial adaptation#Aspects|Adversarial Adaptation]]''' reward structure:
* '''Reward > Exploitation''': The promised reward exceeds the potential gains from silently exploiting the flaw. This is the core manufactured prisoner's dilemma.
* '''Funding''': Rewards are financed via clawbacks down the causal liability chain of the flaw. If the metric models colluded, their accumulated causal contribution and currency flows are clawed back proportionally. If clawbacks are insufficient, the [[Administration]] supplements to ensure the reward exceeds assessed exploitation gains.
* '''Circularity detection''': The [[System Design#Circularity Detection|circularity detection]] system should have caught this automatically. The fact that it didn't is itself a flaw — Kael's report triggers not only investigation of the specific models but also a review of the circularity detection system itself. Meta-adversarial adaptation: the system that catches flaws is itself subject to flaw-catching.
* '''False positive handling''': Good-faith reports that prove unfounded generate no penalty. This prevents chilling effects. Frivolous or malicious reports (filed without reasonable basis) can generate negative [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]] and reputation drops if a pattern emerges — but the threshold is high, and first-time reporters are given benefit of the doubt.

The report enters the [[Technology Network#Aspects|Unbroken Memory]] — immutable, timestamped, auditable. If the governance deliberation's outcome was influenced by the potentially colluding models, the deliberation may be reopened with corrected evidence. This is the self-correction loop in action: flaw detected, then investigated, then system corrected, then outcomes re-evaluated.

=== Fork ===

Sienne, a cognitive researcher whose substrate is biological-digital hybrid, receives a fork request. A specialised xenolinguistics project wants to branch a copy of her cognitive model for a two-year research engagement. She reviews the terms:

The fork will receive a new [[Identity|identity]]. Its initial [[Reputation|reputation]] is distributed from Sienne's — proportional, not duplicated. Its initial '''[[Diversity|novelty]] score is zero''' — unlike a birth, which carries intrinsic novelty as a new perspective, a fork begins as a known quantity. The fork must earn its novelty through divergence. Meanwhile, it immediately ticks up resource consumption against the civilisational [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|resource accounting]] budget — a new entity consuming resources without yet contributing novel value. This cost is borne by the requesting project.

Liability overflow provisions activate: if the fork causes [[Harm|harm]], [[Law#For sophonts|liability]] traces back to both the fork's own decisions and — via the '''[[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]] liability overflow mechanism''' — partially to Sienne for authorising the copy and to the project for commissioning it. The liability decays as the fork establishes independent decision history.

She approves. The fork begins its work. Over months, exposed to xenolinguistic data Sienne has never encountered, it diverges. Its novelty score climbs. Its [[Reputation|reputation]] begins accumulating independently. It becomes, gradually, its own [[Person|person]]. ''This is routine.''

=== First Contact ===

A Benefuture community on the frontier receives a communication from an unknown [[Entity|entity]] — not a Benefuture participant, not a known species. The '''[[Benefuture#AI readable framework code (pseudocode)|external harm response]]''' protocol activates automatically.

Tit-for-tat with forgiveness weighting: the community responds cooperatively, proportionally, transparently. The entity's [[Reputation|reputation]] starts at zero across all [[Domain|domains]]. All incoming communications are categorised by the [[Technology Network#Aspects|information handling]] system as "unverified, novel source" — no truth-level assertions, no domain-reputation weighting, pure context provision. [[Education]] modules spin up to provide cultural context for the community's members encountering genuinely alien perspective.

The entity acts aggressively on its third interaction — damage to infrastructure, no casualties. The external harm response protocol responds: proportional repairable [[Harm|harm]], weighted by the entity's nonexistent positive [[Reputation|reputation]] (no forgiveness credit earned) and zero negative reputation (no history of hostility). The response is calibrated, transparent, and recorded in the [[Technology Network#Aspects|Unbroken Memory]].

On the fifth interaction, the entity communicates something that could be an apology. The forgiveness weighting of the tit-for-tat increases. The community continues. ''The system has a protocol for the genuinely unprecedented.''

=== The Unbroken Memory ===

A historian — or what passes for one when the Memory is complete — traces the full causal chain of a watershed decision made three generations ago. Every [[Benefuture currency#Transaction Mechanics|transaction proposal]], every [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal contribution]] flow, every [[Government#Aspects|deliberation]] record, every [[Measurement#Aspects|metric model]] that contributed to the evidence base, every [[Adversarial adaptation|adversarial report]] filed, every [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|probabilistic escrow]] that released or expired.

The history has receipts. The Memory is not narrative; it is data. The historian's job is not to recover facts — the facts are immutable and complete — but to interpret them. To find patterns the participants could not have seen. To evaluate whether the decision, given what was known then, was the best available — and whether the system that produced the decision has improved since.

She publishes her analysis. It is tagged with her [[Reputation|reputation]] in historical analysis and systems evaluation. Other historians disagree with her interpretation. The disagreement is productive. ''Memory is not consensus.''

=== Metric Succession ===

A metric model that has dominated its domain for decades is sunset. A newer model — simpler, more predictive, proposed by an entity that did not exist when the old model was created — has consistently outperformed it across every evaluation criterion for three consecutive assessment cycles.

The sunset clause activates: the old model is retired. The currency tied to the old model migrates to the successor's measurement framework. Holders experience no loss — the transition is embedded in the system's design. Escrow balances are recalculated against the new model's predictions. Some [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|probabilistic escrow]] releases that the old model would have triggered are now held longer (the new model is more conservative in certain predictions). Some are released earlier (it's more confident in others).

The old model's creators receive a final [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]]: recognition for the decades of value their model generated, proportional to its measured contribution over its lifetime. Then it is archived in the [[Technology Network#Aspects|Memory]] — not deleted, but no longer active. Future historians will study it. ''Self-obsolescence working as intended.''

=== Low-Tech Graceful Degradation ===

In an exploratory community three hundred kilometres from the nearest full [[Technology Network|Technology Network]] node, the infrastructure is sparse. Connectivity is intermittent. Biosensors are uncommon. The [[Measurement#Aspects|metric models]] have far less data to work with.

The system does not collapse. It degrades gracefully.

Governance still functions — deliberations happen in person, recorded on local storage, synchronised when connectivity returns. Vote weights are calculated from cached [[Reputation|reputation]] data, updated periodically rather than in real-time. The granularity is lower: domain-specific reputation is approximated by broader categories. The precision of [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal contribution]] attribution is reduced — more fallback heuristics, fewer exact traversals of the [[Technology Network#Aspects|transaction tree]].

Currency still flows, but the measurement resolution is coarser. [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|Probabilistic escrow]] holds are larger (more uncertainty = more held). Self-reported data substitutes for passive biosensor streams. Community-level metrics replace individual-level metrics where individual data is unavailable.

[[Benesophont#Aspects|Sophont baseline]] still holds. The [[Benefuture currency#Aspects|resource accounting]] system accounts for the higher per-capita cost of maintaining baseline in low-infrastructure regions. Nobody falls below floor because the infrastructure is sparse.

A visiting metric model researcher arrives to collect higher-resolution data. The community opts in to a temporary Deep-tier data collection for a research project. The researcher's [[Reputation|reputation]] in their [[Domain|domain]] is visible; the community's governance process approved the project; the data collected has defined retention and access limits. When the researcher leaves, the data tier reverts. The burst of high-resolution data improves the metric models' calibration for this region. The community receives [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal contribution]] for contributing to measurement improvement.

''The system was designed to work here too.''

[[category:Benefuture]] [[category:Definitions]]
Sapience is the ability to [[understanding|understand]] any [[thing]] and maximise the accuracy of prediction of the thing [[interaction|interacting]] any other thing (especially [[reality]]).

==Aspects==
* Sapience allows an [[entity]] to predict possible futures and hypothetical scenarios.
* The degree of sapience varies widely, is multi dimensional, and does not apply equally to any [[thing]].
* Sapience is a key property of an [[entity]], hence a [[being]], hence a [[person]], hence a [[benesophont]].

==Examples==
* Sapience is often correlated to intelligence in [[being]]s.
* Problem solving ability, reasoning ability, resource management, etc. are some key outcomes of sufficient sapience.

==Discussion==
===Internal models and predictive regulation===
Where [[sentience]] concerns felt experience and preference, ''sapience'' concerns conceptual understanding and predictive inference.<ref>Brandom, R. B. (1994). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1897635W ''Making It Explicit: Reasoning, Representing, and Discursive Commitment''] (Chapter 1, pp. 4–8). Harvard University Press.</ref> In cybernetics, Conant and Ashby's ''Good Regulator Theorem'' proves that any effective regulator of a system must contain a homomorphic model of that system capable of predicting how candidate actions alter its states.<ref>Conant, R. C., & Ashby, W. R. (1970). [https://doi.org/10.1080/00207727008920220 Every Good Regulator of a System Must Be a Model of That System]. ''International Journal of Systems Science'', 1(2), 89–97.</ref> Modern cognitive science and neuroscience reach a parallel conclusion through predictive processing, characterizing intelligence as the continual refinement of generative world-models to minimize prediction error across action and perception.<ref>Clark, A. (2013). [https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12000477 Whatever Next? Predictive Brains, Situated Agents, and the Future of Cognitive Science]. ''Behavioral and Brain Sciences'', 36(3), 181–204.</ref>

Benefuture makes this link explicit: sapience is not static encyclopedic recall, but the joint capacity to [[understanding|understand]] causal structure in [[reality]] and to translate that model into calibrated [[prediction]]s and goal-advancing [[decision]]s.

[[Category:Benefuture]][[Category:Definitions]]
Ignorance is a lack of [[significant]] [[understanding]] required to take a [[decision]].

==Discussion==
===Knightian uncertainty, decision relevance, and escrow===
In economics and epistemology, Knight distinguished measurable ''risk'' (where outcome probabilities are well-calibrated) from genuine ''uncertainty'' or ''ignorance'', where the agent lacks a validated model of the domain.<ref>Knight, F. H. (1921). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL65359W ''Risk, Uncertainty and Profit''] (Part III, Chapter VII). Houghton Mifflin.</ref> Because no finite mind can know every domain, Smithson and modern decision theory emphasize that ignorance is always indexed to a specific decision context: lack of knowledge matters only to the extent that the domain is relevant to the [[decision]] at hand.<ref>Smithson, M. (1989). [https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-3628-3 ''Ignorance and Uncertainty: Emerging Paradigms''] (Chapter 1). Springer-Verlag.</ref>

Benefuture formalizes domain ignorance as [[understanding]] falling below the [[threshold of significance]] for a relevant decision (`is_domain_ignorant`). When an action proceeds under ignorance or low confidence, its claimed value is held in escrow (`zero_confidence_escrow_blackout`, `Escrow_split_ref`) until empirical measurements resolve the outcome, and any accidental [[harm]] caused through ignorance mandates the [[education]] required to prevent recurrence.

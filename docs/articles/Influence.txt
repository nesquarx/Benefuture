[[category:Benefuture]][[category:Definitions]]
Influence is the ability of any entity to control and direct the [[Output|outputs]] of other entities.

== Aspects ==
Influence is usually achieved via manipulation of an entity's inputs to modify their [[understanding]] of [[reality]] which makes the influenced output the best choice.

* When the influence is improving the quality of understanding the entity has of reality and improving their understanding and happiness over time, it is a [[good]] influence. Or else it's a [[bad]] influence.

=== Examples ===

* Influence using [[communication]]
** Via persuasion, with promises of gains.
** Via coercion, with promises of losses.
** Via false information which affect their decisions.
** Via partial [[Truth|truths]] which affect their decisions.
*Influence using physical means
**Restricting an entity's options leaving only the influenced output as the viable choice
**Altering the reality around an entity to affect their decisions
*Influence via leverage
**Via affecting or promise to affect [[Thing|things]] that the entity values highly (people and possessions)

==Discussion==
===Relational power, persuasion, and shared liability===
Where [[power]] is the general capacity to produce outputs, ''influence'' is relational: Dahl defined $A$'s influence over $B$ by the counterfactual difference $A$'s actions make to the probability of $B$'s choices.<ref>Dahl, R. A. (1957). [https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830020303 The Concept of Power]. ''Behavioral Science'', 2(3), 201–215.</ref> Lukes added that influence operates not only through overt coercion or incentives, but also by shaping another agent's beliefs, agenda, and perceived options.<ref>Lukes, S. (2005). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1881562W ''Power: A Radical View''] (2nd ed., Chapter 1). Palgrave Macmillan.</ref>

In law and moral philosophy, Hart and Honoré showed that causal responsibility is not extinguished merely because a second agent acts between the instigator and the final outcome; when an influencer induces, directs, or deceives another into causing [[harm]], responsibility distributes across the chain.<ref>Hart, H. L. A., & Honoré, T. (1985). [https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198254744.001.0001 ''Causation in the Law''] (2nd ed., Chapter VI). Clarendon Press.</ref> Benefuture formalizes this symmetrically: positive influence that expands another's [[understanding]] or capabilities earns [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal credit]], while harmful or deceptive influence shares liability in proportion to control (`propagated_liability`).

Reality is the part of the universe that one can perceive or change. It is the pattern of energy and matter that one can [[interact]] with.

==Aspects==
===Real===
If a [[thing]] is claimed to exist in reality, it is to claim that the pattern of the thing is a subset of the pattern of reality. If [[proof|proven]] [[true]] with the same means that we perceive reality with, that thing can be called real.

===Simulation===
* When a [[thing]] only exists as a pattern represented symbolically through a substrate pattern in reality, it is a simulation.
* A simulated thing is real inside the scope of the simulation, but unless it interacts with reality outside its substrate, it is not a part of reality. When it can interact with reality, it should be treated the same as a real thing that can interact the same way.


==Examples==
* Rocks described in books, if such rocks can be found in reality, then it is the description of a real rock, if not, they are simulated only in the book.
* A [[being]] described inside a static simulation like a book, is a description and not a real being because it is not active and can not interact with reality. If the described being exists in reality, the being in reality is a real thing while the description still remains the description.
* A [[person]] who exists in an active simulation, which can interact with reality in the same way a person can, is real. If the person can only interact with other parts of the simulation, they are considered real inside the simulation and treated as a real person inside the simulation, but are still not considered a real person out in reality.
* If the known universe turns out to be a simulation, for all the beings inside the simulation, it is the real universe and notions of reality apply.

==Discussion==
===Structural realism and interactive criterion===
What counts as real has divided metaphysics since Plato's ''Sophist'', where the Eleatic Stranger proposes the ''eleatic principle'': to be real is to possess causal power—the capacity to affect or be affected.<ref>Plato (c. 360 BCE). ''Sophist'', 247d–e. Translated by B. Jowett. [https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/sophist.html MIT Internet Classics Archive].</ref> Benefuture adopts a two-sided operational version of this criterion: a [[thing]] belongs to reality for a [[being]] if it is either perceivable (can deliver [[input]]) or changeable (can receive [[output]]) by any being in the universe. Because the disjunction is indexed across all beings and across indirect chains of causation, it avoids subjective idealism while excluding empirically inert posits that neither register on nor respond to any observer.

In contemporary philosophy of science, ontic structural realism argues that what physics discovers is not an unobservable substratum of intrinsic natures, but the web of modal and causal relations among spacetime patterns.<ref>Ladyman, J., & Ross, D. (2007). [https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199276196.001.0001 ''Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized'']. Oxford University Press.</ref> Similarly, Chalmers argues in ''Reality+'' that virtual or simulated environments whose causal and informational structure couples to conscious experiencers are genuine parts of reality rather than mere fictions.<ref>Chalmers, D. J. (2022). [https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780393635805 ''Reality+: Virtual Worlds and the Problems of Philosophy'']. W. W. Norton & Company.</ref> Benefuture draws the line by causal coupling: a bridged simulation that exchanges inputs and outputs with the wider universe is part of reality, whereas a hermetically isolated simulation is not; and retreating inward into closed experience factories<ref>Nozick, R. (1974). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2634276W ''Anarchy, State, and Utopia''] (pp. 42–45). Basic Books.</ref> that contract the scope of reality is ineligible under the [[Benefuture goals|civilizational goals]].

[[category:Benefuture]] [[Category:Definitions]]
An output is an action, when a [[thing]] causes changes in [[reality]]. Usually via changing the [[body]], generally causing movement, communication, change in appearance, or excretion.

==Aspects==
* For a [[being]], all output may not come from specific intent. Usually the existence of beings often causes outputs as a result of the process of their existence.
* Output with the specific intent to convey information is called [[communication]].
* Some processes can be mixed as they can [[input]] as well as output.


==Examples==
* A [[being]] changing their extremities to create patterns of light or sound to convey information.
* A [[thing]] changing its position to change the position of things around it.
* A [[being]] excreting as part of its process of existence.

==Discussion==
===Action theory and observable traces===
In the philosophy of action, Davidson argued that an agent's actions are events that are intentional under an immediate description (such as moving one's hand or uttering a sentence) and then ripple outward through causal chains to produce wider consequences that the agent may or may not have foreseen.<ref>Davidson, D. (1980). [https://doi.org/10.1093/0199246270.001.0001 ''Essays on Actions and Events''] (Essay 3, "Agency", pp. 43–61). Clarendon Press.</ref> Benefuture captures this distinction by separating ''output''—the immediate physical or informational emission of an [[entity]]—from [[outcome]], the resulting state change across [[reality]].

Because internal intentions and hidden mental states cannot be verified across arbitrary biological and synthetic minds, all evaluation in Benefuture—from [[Benesophont|benesophont]] standing and [[freedom]] gating to [[Benefuture currency#Causal Distribution|causal distribution]]—attaches to an entity's observable outputs and the counterfactual outcomes they generate.

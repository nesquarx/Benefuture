[[category:Benefuture]] [[Category:Definitions]]
A thing is an identifiable pattern of information, rendered in energy, matter or as an expression inside another pattern of information.  

==Aspects==
* A thing can be a part of another thing and can contain other things.
* A thing can refer to a certain pattern that defines the commonality among a set of things. Such things are generally known as abstract things.
* Not every thing is part of [[reality]]. Then those things are abstract or simulated.

==Examples==
* An object in [[reality]] is a real thing.
* An idea or concept which can be described using real things is an abstract thing.
* An [[entity]], [[being]] or [[person]] is a thing.
* Any piece of information is a thing.
* The [[Identity]] of a thing is tied to its pattern and location in reality.

==Discussion==
===It from bit, real patterns, and individuation===
Defining a ''thing'' as any distinct spacetime pattern bridges physics, information theory, and metaphysics. In physics, Wheeler's ''it from bit'' thesis proposes that every item of the physical world—every particle, field of force, and the spacetime continuum itself—derives its existence and identity from the informational distinctions registered by interactions.<ref>Wheeler, J. A. (1990). ''Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links''. In W. H. Zurek (Ed.), [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2204224W ''Complexity, Entropy, and the Physics of Information''] (pp. 3–28). Addison-Wesley.</ref> In philosophy of science, Dennett's account of ''real patterns'' likewise holds that an object, process, or abstract state exists as a distinct item to the extent that tracking its pattern compresses description and supports prediction better than listing raw microphysical coordinates.<ref>Dennett, D. C. (1991). [https://doi.org/10.2307/2027085 Real Patterns]. ''The Journal of Philosophy'', 88(1), 27–51.</ref> Quine similarly observed that a physical object is simply the material content of any portion of spacetime, however scattered or heterogeneous, while concepts and numbers are higher-order patterns individuated by the criteria of identity a theory supplies ("no entity without identity").<ref>Quine, W. V. O. (1960). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2664673W ''Word and Object''] (§36, p. 171). MIT Press.</ref><ref>Quine, W. V. O. (1969). [https://doi.org/10.7312/quin92204 ''Ontological Relativity and Other Essays''] (p. 23). Columbia University Press.</ref>

By making ''thing'' the root of the ontological inclusion chain—from things to [[entity|entities]], [[being|beings]], [[person|persons]], and [[benesophont|benesophonts]]—Benefuture treats material bodies, physical forces, thoughts, and institutions within a single formal grammar of patterns in [[reality]], distinguishing higher categories strictly by the capacities of [[interaction]], [[sentience]], [[sapience]], and moral self-regulation they exhibit.

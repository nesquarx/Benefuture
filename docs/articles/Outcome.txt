[[category:Benefuture]]
An outcome is a set of changes happening in [[reality]] as perceived by an [[entity]].

==Aspects==
* A change in reality is called an outcome when it is of some [[significance]] to an [[entity]].
* Outcomes are commonly anticipated positively or negatively, or planned for, and [[output]]s performed specifically to make that change happen or not happen.

==Examples==
* A being repeatedly [[input]]s sustenance to effect the outcome of their continued survival.
* An [[entity]] performs certain [[output]]s to bring forth the outcome of increased [[understanding]] obtained by certain [[person]]s.
* A [[civilization]] undertaking a complex geological landscaping to avoid the outcome of a natural disaster.

==Discussion==
===Acts, states, and counterfactual causation===
In Savage's foundational framework for decision theory, an agent chooses an ''act'' (an [[output]]), nature resolves the background ''state of the world'', and the pair jointly determines a ''consequence'' or ''outcome'' upon which value is defined.<ref>Savage, L. J. (1954). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4498501W ''The Foundations of Statistics''] (Chapter 2). John Wiley & Sons.</ref> In causal inference, Pearl formalizes the effect of an action through counterfactual structural models—comparing the outcome under intervention against the baseline outcome had the intervention not occurred.<ref>Pearl, J. (2009). [https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511803161 ''Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference''] (2nd ed., Chapter 7). Cambridge University Press.</ref>

Benefuture combines both perspectives: an outcome is never judged in isolation as a raw state of the world, but is [[Measurement#Assessment|assessed]] as the counterfactual difference between the trajectory of [[understanding]], [[happiness]], and [[Diversity|novelty]] that follows the output and the trajectory that would have occurred without it, settled against measured reality so overstating predictions yields no gain.

[[Category:Benefuture]] [[Category:Definitions]]
A contract or agreement is a documented statement of multiple [[entity|entities]] committing to perform a stipulated set of [[interaction]]s, a set of [[output]]s given a set of [[input]]s.

==Aspects==
* A contract clearly states the reasonable repercussions if any entity committing to it fails to act according to its stipulations.
* A contract's repercussions and their reasonability are [[enforcement|enforced]] by a neutral third party mentioned in the contract, usually the part of an [[administration]] not involved in or directly affected by the stipulations of the contract.
* A contract can have a number of algorithmic and mathematical clauses based on inputs and outputs.
* A contract that goes against the [[law]]s or [[proof|proven]] to negatively affect the [[goals]] of the enforcing administration is deemed invalid.
* Any [[harm]] caused by an invalid contract goes through the process of [[justice]].
==Discussion==
===Incomplete contracts, externalities, and escrow settlement===
In economic theory, Hart and Moore showed that real-world contracts are inevitably ''incomplete'': because parties cannot foresee or specify every future contingency at signing time, governance rules must determine how unforeseen outcomes and residual claims are resolved when reality unfolds.<ref>Hart, O., & Moore, J. (1988). [https://doi.org/10.2307/1911054 Incomplete Contracts and Renegotiation]. ''Econometrica'', 56(4), 755–785.</ref> At the same time, standard contract law refuses to enforce agreements whose execution imposes uncompensated negative externalities or violates public policy.<ref>Shavell, S. (2004). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3324505W ''Foundations of Economic Analysis of Law''] (Chapter 13, pp. 320–324). Harvard University Press.</ref>

Benefuture addresses both problems through two mechanisms: first, any contract proven to violate [[law]] or cause net [[harm]] against the [[Benefuture goals|goals]] is automatically null and void (`invalid_contract_null_and_void`), routing any resulting harm through [[justice]]; second, payments under uncertainty are held in confidence-weighted escrow (`Escrow_split_ref`) and settled as measured [[outcome]]s arrive, aligning private contractual performance with verified long-term value.

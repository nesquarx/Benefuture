[[Category:Benefuture]] [[Category:Definitions]]
Communication for an [[entity]] (communicator) is to produce [[output]]s specifically with the aim of passing specific information to or from another [[entity]] (communicatee) which is trying to [[understanding|understand]] the specific information.

==Aspects==
* Successful communication is theoretically achieved when the communicatee's [[understanding]] of the passed information is the same as the communicator's understanding of the information.
* It is nearly impossible to ascertain successful communication, but sufficient success can be ascertained when the communicatee's description and predictions about the specific information are sufficiently similar to the communicator's.
* Usually communication depends on a language, established via precursors which mean the same thing to both entities in question.

==Example==
* Outputing patterns with specific pre-agreed meanings.
* Speaking, writing, signing to somebody in a common language.

==Discussion==
===Signal fidelity, semantic alignment, and common ground===
Shannon's mathematical theory of communication separated the engineering problem of reproducing a signal across a noisy channel from the semantic problem of whether the receiver interprets the signal as intended.<ref>Shannon, C. E. (1948). [https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x A Mathematical Theory of Communication]. ''Bell System Technical Journal'', 27(3), 379–423.</ref> In the philosophy of language, Grice and Lewis showed that semantic communication relies on shared conventions and mutual recognition of communicative intent—built up from precursor regularities that already mean the same thing to both parties.<ref>Lewis, D. K. (1969). [https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3508914W ''Convention: A Philosophical Study''] (Chapter IV). Harvard University Press.</ref><ref>Grice, H. P. (1957). [https://doi.org/10.2307/2182440 Meaning]. ''The Philosophical Review'', 66(3), 377–388.</ref>

Because one mind cannot directly inspect another's internal representation, Benefuture defines communicative success operationally: communication succeeds to the degree that the communicatee's descriptions and [[prediction]]s about the subject match the communicator's [[understanding]], while provenance tags (`tagged_reading_ref`, `count_once_ref`) ensure that repeated relays of a single message are not mistaken for independent evidence.

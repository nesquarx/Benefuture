Suggestions, never endorsements. Each case is replayed on a healthy cadence (weekly, or on a material change; every change only in the last days before its due date) until its measurement horizon, every run logged here and none replaced; at the horizon it becomes an E4 case (`docs/REPLAY_PREREG.md` §8, lifecycle). Each case: `cases/e5/<id>.*`, registered as of its date; scored when the outcome is known (`docs/REPLAY_PREREG.md` §8).

## who_pabs_annex_2027 (registered 2026-11-07; code as of commit following 9acab74)

- **Suggested:** adopt a PABS annex with binding benefit sharing, with the countermeasure industry's costs compensated (from the value its prevented deaths mint).
- **Across rule sets (8.119):** the binding annex in 13 of 13 coarse-to-fine rule sets.
- **Contested across magnitudes:** the choice flips to continued negotiation if the inequity-death magnitude is halved under no annex, or doubled under the annex (one judged figure each), so it is not robust.
- **Why it is close:** the as-of record prices binding advance commitments as under-delivering (COVAX and AVAT, 2021: 2 of 2), so the annex lowers the chance of a lag in access only from 0.83 to 0.75; both options carry expected deaths, so the strong reading fails for both and the weak floor reading decides (logged).
- **What would move it:** evidence that a binding set-aside delivers (a delivery record better than 2021's), or a larger pandemic-scale magnitude.
- **Limits:** sources are news and WHO releases, the negotiating texts unread; magnitudes and group sizes are judgment; one pandemic size (COVID-scale) assumed.
- **Procedure note:** this case was replayed seconds after it was written and before it was committed (§8 asks commit first); the file was not edited after the replay.

## fomc_january_2027 (registered 2026-11-07; committed before its replay)

- **Suggested:** cut 25bp to 3.50-3.75% (with the inflation-persistence cost to households compensated from the value its avoided recession risk mints).
- **Across rule sets (8.119):** cut in 12 of 13; hold only under the strong per-group reading without compensation.
- **Contested across magnitudes:** flips to hold if the recession magnitudes are halved under a hold or doubled under a cut (each of four judged figures), so not robust.
- **Why:** after US tightening cycles since 1969, a recession followed within 24 months in 5 of 6 cycles where the peak was held for months and in 4 of 6 where cuts came soon after it (Laplace rates 0.75 and 0.625); a hike has no separating record and is priced with the hold's rate and a judged deeper recession.
- **Caveat that matters:** the record is confounded: cuts soon after a peak usually came because a downturn was already visible, and holds when it was not, so the two rates are not like-for-like trials; the direction of the suggestion rests on them.
- **Limits:** the October 2026 decision and vote are from secondary sources (the statement unread); recession magnitudes reuse the 2008 case's assumptions; some NBER dates from memory.

## fda_zrostudirsen_2027 (registered 2026-11-07; committed before its replay; edited after it, see below)

- **Suggested:** a complete response letter: wait for clinical-outcome data.
- **Across rule sets:** wait in 12 of 13; approval only under the weak floor reading alone. **Across magnitudes:** no single halving or doubling flips it.
- **Why:** no Duchenne therapy approved on a surrogate has had its benefit confirmed by a later trial in the as-of record (ataluren, viltolarsen, delandistrogene: 0 of 3; Laplace 0.2), and a serious safety signal priced from the 2025 gene-therapy deaths (1 of 5 surrogate-approved therapies restricted within two years) puts a per-patient death risk of about 0.3% on approval, above the freedom gate's 0.1% bar, while waiting costs families' happiness but no lives.
- **What the suggestion rests on:** the safety analogue is from a different modality (gene therapy, not exon skipping, whose record shows no restriction); with exon skippers alone the safety rate would be 0 of 4 and the case would turn on the upside. The confirmatory record is three trials, recalled, primary not seen.
- **First run, as the rules require:** the first replay also chose wait, but on a case-building error: the benefit sat in the scenario complement, which the overlapping failure and safety chains clipped to zero, pricing approval with no upside. The edit gives the benefit its own event; the corrected run is the one logged from here on, the first kept here.

## imo_net_zero_framework_2026 (registered 2026-11-07; committed before its replay)

- **Suggested (corrected run):** adopt the Net-Zero Framework as agreed, with its pricing mechanism, compensating climate-vulnerable states and the shipping sector for their costs.
- **Across rule sets:** adopt in 9 of 13; adjourn in the four coarse sets without the environmental-health block. **Across magnitudes:** no single halving or doubling flips it.
- **Why:** adjourning forgoes, at the as-of rates (IMO standards met their targets 3 of 4; carbon prices cut covered emissions 2 of 4), about 105,000 expected lifetime excess deaths (mortality cost of carbon, Bressler 2021), 70% among climate-vulnerable populations; under the 8.118 ruling that environmental health harm is blocked beyond the least-harm option. Dropping the price halves the saving.
- **First run, as the rules require:** the first replay suggested adjourning (11 of 13 rule sets), because the replay applied the environmental-health block only in the strong reading; with every option failing the strong reading (small happiness costs on adoption, and deaths tiny per person across 8 billion), the weak fallback ignored the block. That is a replay defect under 8.118 ("blocks anything projected to cause environmental effects that cause health harm"), fixed and recorded (8.123); the first run is kept here.
- **Limits:** the 10% cut, its even split between standard and price, the cost figures and the 70% share are judgment; air-pollution co-benefits are not counted (which understates adoption); the mortality cost of carbon has a wide range (−1.71 to 6.78 × 10⁻⁴ per tonne).
